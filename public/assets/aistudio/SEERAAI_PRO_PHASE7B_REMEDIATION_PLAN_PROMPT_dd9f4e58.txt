TASK B — DESIGN SAFE SHARED-DEVICE SIGN-OUT AND ACCOUNT-SWITCH HANDLING

2. Prevent User B from viewing User A's local drafts, cached titles, chat history, or offline queue after account switch or sign-out.
3. On sign-out, first detect pending unsynchronized changes. Never silently discard a user's only copy. Specify a safe user flow: sync successfully before cleanup; if sync fails, explain the pending-data risk and provide explicit “stay signed in and retry” versus “discard pending local changes and sign out” choices. 4. Handle abrupt browser close, expired auth session, revoked session, and account switch—not just clicking a sign-out button.
5. On next login, verify cache ownership against the authenticated UID before loading any user-specific local data. Treat missing/mismatched ownership metadata as unsafe and do not display that cache.
6. Public legal materials may remain cached only if they are genuinely public and contain no user-private or institutional data.
7. Do not cache confidential institutional records, evidence, draft judgments, internal audit logs, or non-public case files in shared-device persistent storage. If current offline features depend on this, describe the compatibility impact and propose a safe alternative without deleting the feature.

If encryption is proposed, explain threat model, key lifecycle, recovery, XSS limitations, and why it is stronger than UID scoping plus purge. Prefer minimizing/purging sensitive cache.
